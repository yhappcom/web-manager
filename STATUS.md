## 108 material findings
- a narrow recovery parser/converter is security-critical infrastructure: reducing legacy authority concentrates trust in the remaining bridge rather than eliminating supply-chain risk;
- distinguish exact artifact identity, provenance authenticity, accepted builder/signer, dependency inventory/resolution, vulnerability/applicability state and semantic historical-recovery correctness;
- SLSA-style provenance can establish how an artifact was produced and bind it to a builder/source/build definition, but does not prove a dependency is non-malicious or a converter preserves domain semantics;
- SBOM/dependency inventory is useful only when consumed against advisories, vulnerability/exploitability and support/EOL evidence; dependency risk changes after release;
- bind supply-chain and recovery evidence to the same artifact: converter digest × provenance/builder × dependency resolution × corpus/oracle identity × environment × result;
- historical regression corpus is itself governed evidence. Prefer synthetic/minimized fixtures and independent semantic oracles; real user backups are not default regression fixtures;
- patch/replace vulnerable bridges through opaque-artifact preservation, exact-artifact provenance, full historical/adversarial corpus, current-domain reconciliation and authority revalidation rather than package upgrade alone;
- failure of provenance/dependency/semantic admission should remove privileged recovery publication/replay authority while preserving candidate user data.
