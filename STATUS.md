## Current integrated checkpoint — through 282
- Composite `NORMAL` remains derived/revocable; historical/cached decisions do not become independent trust roots.
- Assurance independence is threat/consequence-specific; budget exhaustion is consequence-specific rather than source-count based.
- Explicit conceptual states include `NORMAL`, `DEGRADED`, `UNKNOWN`, `CONTAINED`, `RECOVERY` and evidence-based re-entry. These are not prescribed product enums.
- Graceful assurance degradation is capability decomposition, not `NORMAL` with a warning. Local read/capture/preservation may remain defensible while remote publication/destructive mutation/stale recovery authority is fenced, subject to product validation.
- **Degraded-mode policy is consequence-bearing authority.** The mapping from assurance state/consequence to allowed capability requires lineage/currentness and cannot be treated as a UI/config flag.
- **Capability grants are non-transitive by default.** Local capture, queueing, export or read permission does not compose into remote publication/destructive mutation authority.
- Consequence classification cannot be trusted merely because a client or legacy compatibility adapter labels an operation low-risk; material boundaries must derive/validate current consequence semantics.
- Cached `NORMAL`, cached degraded capability matrices and prior allow decisions are derived/revocable and must not survive a security-relevant policy-generation transition merely because TTL remains.
- Restore/PITR is a policy-currentness mutation event. An authentic older permissive policy cannot self-elect as current from the same rolled-back state.
- Service Worker activation/update is not organizational policy migration and cannot establish authority. Long-offline clients may retain stale runtime/policy hints while unique data remains valuable.
- Recovery state is not a universal super-role. Recovery-only capability requires bounded scope, provenance and retirement/negative proof.
- Re-entry requires both assurance-basis recovery and current policy-generation proof, including predecessor-policy negative tests, stale-cache invalidation, PITR anti-rollback and long-offline rejoin evidence.
