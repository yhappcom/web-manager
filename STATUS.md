## 127 checkpoint
- Recovery independence is scenario-relative and must be evaluated across the end-to-end dependency graph, not by counting providers, accounts, copies or regions.
- Identity tenant, administrator, authenticator/device, recovery mailbox, registrar/DNS, source/CI, hosting/CDN, key custody, billing/legal ownership, network path and evidence sink can create hidden common-mode cut sets.
- A nominal alternate can be independent for hosting failure while still collapsing under the same SSO/operator failure.
- Independence has administrative, identity, provider/control-plane, data-copy, geographic/network, software/toolchain, human-role, temporal and evidence dimensions; do not compress them into one boolean.
- Full active-active multi-provider duplication is not the default. Bounded manual rebuild plus independently protected domain, source/release/export, credentials and tested recovery may be stronger and simpler for moderate consequence.
- Central provider/origin recovery and installed-PWA recovery are separate graphs; provider failover does not update a long-offline iPad or authorize its stale outbox.
- Provider-specific upstream/control-plane/recovery facts remain CHANGE WATCH and OPEN until verified for selected services.
- Track C now owns destructive common-mode drills that deliberately remove SSO, people, devices, registrar/DNS, source/CI, hosting, evidence and backup dependencies.
- Dependency independence claims become stale after material SSO/provider/domain/CI/key-custody changes and require change-triggered re-evaluation.
