## 161 checkpoint
- Closure evidence is bound to distinct lineage, canonicalization, digest, signature/key, trust-policy and verifier generations rather than one opaque crypto version.
- Planned rotation and compromise are different protocols: compromise requires a defensible historical-validity interval rather than ordinary rollover semantics.
- RFC 5280 `invalidityDate` and revocation processing time are distinct; detection/revocation time must not be invented as the exact compromise start.
- Historical evidence can be `PRE-COMPROMISE-ANCHORED`, `SUSPECT-WINDOW`, `POST-COMPROMISE/REJECTED` or `UNVERIFIABLE` according to evidence; mathematical signature validity alone is insufficient.
- Re-signing/migrating historical evidence creates a new attestation/transformation event and cannot recreate original historical legitimacy.
- Trusted temporal evidence can help bound proof-of-existence where architecture requires it, but local/self-asserted time and timestamp presence alone are insufficient.
- Historical verifier/public-key retention may support verify-only archival claims without preserving historical private/current authority.
- Algorithm/canonicalization migration preserves the original committed representation plus authenticated transformation lineage; silent deserialize/re-serialize/re-sign is not original-proof preservation.
- Legacy verifier capability is bounded/isolated and cannot let input select weaker current policy.
- Long-offline PWA state may carry old closure/verifier context but cannot choose current server trust; unique local data remains preservable/exportable even if proof confidence degrades.
- NIST CSWP 39upd1 is current final crypto-agility guidance; SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev.6 and SP 800-131A Rev.3 remain CHANGE WATCH drafts in the current NIST index.
