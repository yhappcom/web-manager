## 110 material findings
- recovery escrow independence is a failure-domain property, not a copy-count property. Storage/provider, identity/SSO, authorization, key/trust, network/control-plane, billing/domain ownership, personnel/tooling and physical hazards must be evaluated separately;
- different repositories, regions or providers can still share one destructive trust path. Maintain a failure-domain matrix rather than an `independent=true` label;
- preserve non-secret representation knowledge, synthetic corpus/oracles, provenance/fixity and reconstruction procedure separately from production secrets, live signing authority and real user data;
- key recoverability is purpose-specific: long-lived decryption may require recovery while current signing/private authority must not be casually bundled into preservation escrow;
- organizational survivability requires executable succession: a documented alternate role/path and a drill performed by someone other than the original author, without relying on the primary identity/control plane;
- custody migration requires source/destination fixity plus authenticity/custody transition evidence; checksum equality alone does not authenticate custody history;
- strongest generic exercise removes the primary repository/cloud/SSO and original converter executable, then locates/authenticates the independent escrow, reconstructs under current tooling and passes 108/109 semantic/provenance gates;
- destructive authority deserves separate treatment from read/reconstruction authority. Exact dual-control/quorum policy remains product-risk dependent;
- privacy minimization remains binding: organizational survivability does not justify turning escrow into a durable aggregation of production keys, credentials or personal records.
