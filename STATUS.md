## Current integrated checkpoint — through 269
- Composite `NORMAL` is a derived, revocable decision, not a new independent trust root; cache reuse retains prerequisite/epoch/topology/revocation dependencies.
- Historical decision validity and current applicability are separate. Revocation preserves historical evidence while blocking current affected consequences.
- Revocation/contradiction propagates through actual dependency edges to affected composites and downstream consequences, not by timestamp/device/incident association alone.
- Decision caches require semantic context such as subject/incarnation, consequence, composition-policy generation, relevant authority/policy/schema/configuration epochs, prerequisite/verifier context, topology generation and revocation generation; TTL alone is insufficient.
- Push/broadcast invalidation is an optimization. Long-offline PWA safety requires authoritative stale-operation rejection when a queued operation eventually arrives.
- Queued payload/provenance can remain valuable after execution authority expires; `queue retained ≠ queue authorized`.
- Service Worker/controller/Cache Storage currentness is runtime evidence, not semantic authorization or revocation closure.
- Browser caches are only one surface; API/resource-server/policy-engine/job/manual/admin decision caches require dependency-aware invalidation too.
- Backup/PITR/reimport must not roll current revocation/decision state backward; post-restore reconciliation or governed rebootstrap is required.
- Late-returning offline clients preserve unique data, fence auto-replay/high-consequence effects and construct successor composite evidence under current prerequisites rather than inheriting fleet NORMAL.
- Contradiction can force affected cached decisions into revalidation before root cause is fully known; this does not prove global compromise.
- Reauthorization creates successor evidence rather than rewriting the revoked historical decision.
