## 156 checkpoint
- A recovery ceremony is a versioned authorization protocol with humans in the loop, not merely a meeting/checklist or quorum count.
- Consequential approval binds incident + target + package/checkpoint + requested consequence + ceremony generation/currentness + governed participant role.
- Participant authentication and recovery authorization are distinct; even phishing-resistant authentication does not establish that the incident/package/consequence is legitimate.
- NIST SP 800-63B-4 is the current final authentication baseline: manual-entry OTP/OOB methods are not phishing-resistant; channel/verifier binding is the relevant protocol property.
- CISA guidance supports phishing-resistant MFA for high-value targets and identifies ordinary push approval as fatigue-prone; number matching mitigates push bombing but does not become phishing-resistant.
- `out-of-band` is not treated as `independent` when channels share the same compromised SSO, endpoint, provider, directory or control plane.
- Ceremony evidence is minimized and integrity-preserved without storing recovery shares, OTPs, bearer tokens or reusable approval credentials.
- Retained approval/audit artifacts remain non-executable through ceremony/target/currentness/consumption semantics.
- Long-offline PWA/iPad state can preserve irreplaceable data but cannot establish participant legitimacy or organizational recovery authority.
