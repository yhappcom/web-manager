## 119 material findings
- incident closure returns the recovered system to ongoing cybersecurity risk management; it does not erase residual uncertainty;
- a normalized post-incident baseline is an explicit current trust/authority contract, not merely removal of emergency controls or an incident banner;
- distinguish `resolved/validated`, `accepted residual risk`, `open remediation/validation`, and `unknown/unclassifiable`; age or ticket closure must not collapse these states;
- risk acceptance is an intentional, attributable governance response to a defined risk; missing fixes/tests/owners are unfinished work, not acceptance;
- acceptance should govern bounded exposure without technically re-authorizing revoked credentials, stale trust/API generations, hostile executable state or unreconciled operations;
- central service can normalize while long-offline EFB clients remain explicitly unknown, provided their next remote mutation must satisfy the current fail-closed re-entry gate;
- compromise-era local data may remain preserved/readable/exportable while integrity is unresolved; usefulness does not establish provenance or replay authority;
- emergency controls that survive recovery must either retire or be deliberately promoted through ordinary ownership/threat/privacy/validation governance;
- residual-risk acceptance needs monitoring and reconsideration triggers; `accepted once` is not `accepted forever`;
- telemetry informs risk review but cannot manufacture fleet convergence or replace the authorization boundary.
