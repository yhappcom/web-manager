## Current integrated checkpoint — through 272
- Composite `NORMAL` remains derived/revocable; cached or historical decisions do not become independent trust roots.
- Planned rollover, predecessor compromise, and predecessor+successor overlap compromise are distinct trust problems.
- If every in-band authority capable of certifying the next state is inside the suspected compromise set, in-band continuity cannot manufacture clean trust.
- Cross-signatures from mutually suspect authorities remain incident/provenance evidence but cannot establish a clean successor by themselves.
- Emergency trust reset is a change of trust basis, not merely a larger generation number or newer timestamp.
- A recovery basis must be evaluated against the actual compromise domain: credentials, administration, deployment, storage, identity/provider/control plane and personnel workflow.
- When no clean in-band authority survives, high-consequence availability may need to degrade while preserve/read/recovery remains available where safe.
- Unique offline data remains distinct from execution authority. Trust reset must not become destructive data reset.
- Late/offline clients converge through authenticated reset lineage or governed rebootstrap; they cannot vote obsolete R1/R2 authority back into existence.
- R3 positive operation does not retire R1/R2. Material retirement requires authoritative rejection/fencing of obsolete consequences across applicable paths.
- Backup/PITR must not resurrect pre-reset authority as current; reset currentness needs an anti-rollback reference outside the restored failure domain or governed rebootstrap.
- Service Worker/MDM/push/API delivery can distribute reset material but cannot itself establish semantic authority.
- Emergency recovery capability is itself a high-value attack surface and must not become an unbounded permanent super-root.
