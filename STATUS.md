## 125 checkpoint
- Recovery is retrieval/reconstruction from authenticated retained sources; replacement keys, same labels, migration or re-signing do not recreate lost historical trust.
- Historical claim states separate `VERIFIED`, `PARTIALLY RECONSTRUCTED`, `UNVERIFIABLE`, `UNKNOWN/GAP` and `INVALID`; inability to verify is neither proof of falsehood nor PASS.
- Mathematical signature verification is narrower than reconstructing historical trust-anchor/policy/authorization context.
- Loss of sink index can preserve individual authenticity while destroying completeness/order assurance; loss of epoch transition can leave adjacent epochs individually valid but continuity unknown.
- Clean-room verifier/parser recovery is valid only from preserved specification/canonicalization plus known-good/known-bad fixtures; missing semantics must not be guessed under today's schema.
- A new recovery authority may sign a reconstruction report, but that authenticates the new report rather than the lost historical producer claim.
- Recovered historical verification material must not reactivate retired signing authority, obsolete API/client generations or stale Service Worker trust.
- For long-offline EFB/PWA, local records may remain useful with explicit provenance uncertainty while current remote mutation re-entry remains fail-closed under current trust/API/reconciliation gates.
