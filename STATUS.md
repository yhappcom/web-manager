## Current integrated checkpoint — through 262
- Distribution success is not authority success; transport/cache freshness cannot elect canonical policy or prove semantic application.
- Consumer monotonic floors/checkpoints are security state; loss/reset/restore requires recovery rather than resetting the floor.
- Partial fleet convergence is explicit assurance debt; offline/unknown tails remain visible until governed disposition.
- Device retirement is an authorized lifecycle transition, not telemetry timeout; same physical hardware after erase/re-enrollment may be a new administrative incarnation.
- Data continuity can be preserved while authority continuity remains intentionally broken.
- Transport retry/idempotency, package/record identity, semantic duplicate detection and conflict resolution are distinct controls.
- Conflict resolution is a provenance-bearing decision; conditional writes prevent blind lost updates but do not elect semantic truth.
- Escalation does not inflate routing/support/admin roles into universal domain authority; reopening/supersession preserves historical decision provenance.
- Downstream revocation is a versioned convergence problem. Source correction, cache invalidation, delivery, receipt, semantic application, durable commit and acknowledgement are distinct states.
- Acknowledgement is bound to recipient/incarnation, subject, projection/floor, consequence and current policy/verifier context. Client self-report is evidence, not authority.
- Credential compromise scopes uncertainty; credential rotation establishes a successor authority epoch rather than re-authoring predecessor history.
- Compromise impact follows typed consequence-bearing dependency edges; missing provenance creates explicit assurance debt rather than proof of safety or universal compromise.
- Reauthorization is new scoped evidence under a successor authority epoch; closure is recomputed from current prerequisites and predecessor-rejection evidence where controllable.
- Dependency-graph consistency is not completeness. Completeness is consequence-scoped and bound to enumerated discovery surfaces, methods, known unknowns and current assumptions.
- Hidden-edge discovery covers technical stores/caches/queues/jobs, exports/integrations, backup/restore, offline fleet tails and manual workflows as applicable; negative evidence is valid only for its explicit search space.
- Reauthorization proofs are bound to graph/policy/verifier/schema/authority/topology assumptions; material changes trigger review even when TTL has not expired.
- Dependency inventories are evidence, not reality. Inventory provenance and discovery-control health must be explicit.
- Declared topology and observed topology are reconciled; contradiction is drift evidence, not automatically compromise and not automatically noise.
- A producer-controlled registry/collector cannot be the sole high-consequence completeness proof. Use an independent-enough challenge path proportionate to risk.
- Independence is dimensional: data source, collection mechanism, credentials, administration, deployment/update path, storage, reviewer and failure domain matter more than vendor count.
- Discovery states distinguish CURRENT, STALE, PARTIAL, FAILED, CONTRADICTED, UNKNOWN and UNDER-CHALLENGE; collector failure is not an empty inventory.
- Material topology-changing events trigger inventory reconciliation; successful deployment/change approval does not prove discovery controls absorbed the change.
- Offline/long-tail PWA devices remain explicit unknown consumers until governed retirement/disposition or bounded rejoin evidence exists.
- If the primary discovery mechanism is compromised, re-running the same mechanism is circular; preserve independent-enough reconstruction/challenge evidence.
- Analytics, Web Push, Service Worker and session freshness can reveal contradiction or accelerate convergence but cannot create semantic acknowledgement, graph completeness or compromise clearance.
