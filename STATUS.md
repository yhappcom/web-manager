## 171–212 integrated checkpoint
- Authority, distribution/provisioning, observation, evidence and convergence remain separate layers; offline-first utility preserves unique local data without making stale authority current.
- Recovery request, ceremony authorization, exceptional-capability activation, execution, normalization and post-recovery privilege contraction remain separate gates.
- Evidence signing and historical evidence verification remain separate capabilities; retaining historical verification context must not preserve current signing/recovery authority.
- **Current cryptographic acceptability and historical verification are separate policy questions.** An algorithm can become unacceptable for new protection while bounded legacy verification remains necessary.
- **Long-horizon preservation adds authenticated migration/re-attestation lineage; it does not rewrite original provenance.** Original evidence and later preservation evidence remain distinguishable.
- **Preservation must occur while predecessor trust is still supportable.** A strong successor wrapper created only after the predecessor protection is breakable cannot retroactively prove continuity.
- **Timestamp/signature renewal and hash/object rebinding solve different aging failures.** If the digest binding the original object becomes weak, wrapping only the old digest/timestamp is insufficient.
- Trust-anchor expiry, compromise, algorithm deprecation and verifier removal remain distinct events with distinct evidence states.
- Re-attestation is evidence about prior evidence and must not upgrade `WINDOW-UNCERTAIN` or unsupported historical claims merely by re-signing them.
- Legacy verification remains read-only/purpose-scoped; obsolete verifier retirement requires proof that required evidence populations are migrated or independently verifiable.
- Long-offline/company-iPad convergence preserves unique data first and obtains current authority without using historical preservation keys as synchronization credentials.
