## Current integrated checkpoint — through 271
- Composite `NORMAL` remains a derived, revocable decision rather than an independent trust root.
- Revocation distribution is evidence transport; currentness needs authoritative source/scope, authenticity, freshness/currentness semantics and anti-rollback relation.
- Planned authority rollover and compromise recovery are different trust problems. A suspected-compromised predecessor cannot be the sole trust basis for declaring its successor clean.
- Successor admission must use a trust path whose assumptions survive the stated compromise model; multiple keys/signatures are not independent when they share a compromised control plane.
- Dual-authority overlap is bounded migration state, not permanent co-equal truth. Transition evidence needs predecessor/successor identity, scope/consequence, generation, precedence and retirement criteria.
- Authority lineage itself is anti-rollback. Once R2 is established current for a scope, authentic historical R1 evidence cannot silently make R1 current again.
- Fleet migration need not be atomic. Central/current systems may advance while late/offline clients remain unqualified; those clients rejoin through forward lineage reconciliation or governed rebootstrap.
- Acceptance/adoption windows are evidence windows, not proof by elapsed time. Product-specific timers must not be cargo-culted from another protocol.
- Successor positive tests do not prove predecessor retirement. Material retirement needs authoritative rejection/fencing of obsolete predecessor consequences across applicable execution paths.
- Unique offline data remains distinct from execution authority. A late client can preserve R1-era records while R1 publication/sync authority is rejected.
- Service Worker/browser update state is runtime evidence, not semantic authority succession.
- Backup/PITR recovery must preserve the authority-transition boundary; restoring pre-rotation state cannot silently resurrect R1 currentness.
