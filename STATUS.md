## 128 checkpoint
- Recovery independence is not permanent after one drill; material identity/provider/domain/CI/key-custody/topology changes can invalidate prior claims.
- Resilience is governed per capability and consequence. A local `resilience budget` records intentional common-mode concentration, compensating controls, evidence, owner and reconsideration triggers; it is not a numeric score or NIST term.
- Shared dependency is not automatically a defect. Bounded concentration can be rational when consequence/manual recovery justify it; silent or newly introduced concentration is the governance failure.
- Dependency claims use change states such as VALID, VALIDATED-AFTER-CHANGE, WEAKENED-ACCEPTED, MITIGATION-REQUIRED, UNKNOWN and STALE.
- Material changes require dependency-diff reasoning: new/removed nodes and edges, authority paths, shared credentials, automation blast radius, provider assumptions and installed-PWA implications.
- Redundancy/diversity can add attack surface, lifecycle cost and inconsistency; active-active multi-provider remains non-default.
- Central PWA dependency migration and installed-client convergence remain separate. A long-offline iPad does not inherit central migration success.
- Track C owns change-triggered destructive validation and negative stale-client oracles; automation can detect some topology drift but cannot prove provider/support/human independence alone.
- Accepted concentration needs reconsideration triggers. A material topology change can reopen risk before a scheduled review date.
