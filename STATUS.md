## 164 checkpoint
- Deletion intent, destruction authority, retention/hold authority, operational disposition and physical propagation are distinct state dimensions.
- Korea PIPA Article 21 provides a concrete current example: unnecessary personal information is destroyed without delay except where another law requires preservation; preserved data under that exception is separately stored/managed. This is source evidence, not a MintTap/LogMate applicability conclusion.
- EU/UK source evidence likewise shows erasure can coexist with legal-obligation/legal-claim retention and restricted processing; exact applicability remains legal-review dependent.
- Browser/PWA code consumes minimum authoritative disposition. Locale, IP, GPS, App Store region or cached Service Worker state do not determine governing legal policy.
- Retention/holds are scoped, versioned, currentness-checked inputs. An active hold suppresses destruction only for justified scope/purpose/lifetime and does not authorize ordinary reuse.
- Hold release is not deletion authority or rollback. It creates a new policy generation and requires re-evaluation of all remaining current obligations before any destruction generation advances.
- Unresolved material policy contradiction enters governed review/unknown rather than a destructive guess; that fail-safe must have ownership/escalation so it does not become indefinite silent retention.
- Restore/PITR must reconcile both later hold activation and later hold release/deletion generations before ordinary service reopens.
- Long-offline iPads preserve unique local data under ambiguity while stale remote lineage/policy is blocked from automatic republish or destruction.

## Persistent guards added by 164
`deletion requested ≠ destruction authorized`.  
`retention required ≠ ordinary processing allowed`.  
`hold active ≠ retain every related byte`.  
`hold released ≠ delete immediately without re-evaluation`.  
`one jurisdiction says retain ≠ every jurisdiction/data scope says retain`.  
`client locale ≠ governing jurisdiction`.  
`device location ≠ sufficient legal applicability fact`.  
`policy input authentic ≠ policy input current`.  
`policy engine produced outcome ≠ legal interpretation proven correct`.  
`restriction/hold ≠ silent normal availability`.  
`last hold removed ≠ pre-hold policy state restored`.
