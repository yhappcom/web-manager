## 135 checkpoint
- Compaction, export, import and merge are provenance transformations, not transparent copies. Derived packages retain their own transformation provenance and do not rewrite original authorship.
- Integrity, authenticity, completeness, confidentiality, interpretability and current authorization are independent assurance dimensions.
- A retained hash/checkpoint can support integrity/equality verification but does not make deleted payload available or prove omitted history complete.
- Portable packages should declare their scoped inventory/range, format, compaction/redaction, integrity metadata and external-content availability rather than imply `all history`.
- Import is current-policy admission into a new trust context: package verification does not itself admit records or restore remote mutation authority.
- Cross-device transfer preserves original operation/event lineage. Same account, destination device or new local record identity does not become historical authorship.
- Divergent offline device histories remain branches until reconciliation; wall-clock ordering and structural merge are insufficient evidence of semantic convergence.
- PWA export/import may be valuable for irreplaceable local data, but `export works`, `file exists`, desktop restore and second-device import are not managed-iPad backup/sync proof.
