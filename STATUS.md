## 152 checkpoint
- Policy-generation retirement is a lifecycle: CURRENT → predecessor DRAINING → COMPATIBILITY-ONLY → RETIRED, with REVOKED and HISTORICAL-VERIFICATION-ONLY states where appropriate.
- Generation support/parseability/migration compatibility are distinct from remote authorization.
- A surviving predecessor transaction is a bounded server-recognized lease, not continued general G1 authority.
- Lease identity binds operation/transaction, significant payload, principal/device/trust context, policy/evaluator generation, capability, authoritative expiry, revocation epoch and completion/consumption state.
- Retries preserve operation/lease identity and cannot renew expiry or obtain a fresh authorization opportunity.
- Emergency compromise/revocation can override ordinary draining leases where consequence requires it; availability pressure cannot resurrect compromised authority.
- Authority horizon, automated compatibility horizon and data-recovery/export horizon are separate. Data recovery may outlive server authority.
- Long-offline PWA expiry preserves local irreplaceable data while remote mutation remains gated; offline duration is not grandfathered authority.
- Client clock, cached policy flags, Service Worker generation and stale runtime cannot extend server-side lease authority.
- NIST SP 800-63B-4 is the current identity guideline revision; superseded 63-3 timeout values must not be treated as current requirements.

## Persistent guards added by 152

`generation parseable ≠ generation executable`.  
`transaction started under G1 ≠ G1 may authorize forever`.  
`lease unexpired ≠ revocation cannot override it`.  
`retry ≠ lease renewal`.  
`client clock before expiry ≠ server lease valid`.  
`compatibility horizon ≠ authority horizon`.  
`local data older than horizon ≠ local data should be deleted`.  
`old Service Worker active ≠ old policy active`.  
`offline duration long ≠ grandfathered remote authority`.

`G1 lease exists ≠ G1 remains generally authoritative`.
