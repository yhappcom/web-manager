## Current integrated checkpoint — through 274
- Composite `NORMAL` remains derived/revocable; historical/cached decisions do not become independent trust roots.
- Planned rollover, predecessor compromise, overlap compromise, recovery-root compromise, and organizational custody loss are distinct trust problems.
- A recovery/break-glass root is itself a high-consequence authority; its operational availability depends on current authorization, custody, authenticators, provider/control plane, procedure and organizational mandate—not key bytes alone.
- Custodian availability, custodian authorization and custodian compromise are separate dimensions.
- Succession is an authority transition, not a password/share handoff; obsolete custodian authority needs retirement/rejection evidence.
- Quorum loss does not authorize silent threshold reduction; numerical quorum does not prove trustworthy quorum.
- A permanently shared hot credential is not a substitute for resilient recovery custody.
- Provider/HSM/KMS/IdP/account loss can be a recovery-custody failure even when key bytes still exist.
- Corporate/control-plane transfer does not automatically transfer emergency mandate.
- Custody generation/currentness must resist backup/PITR and long-offline rollback.
- High-consequence recovery may degrade while unique offline data and safe preserve/read/recovery capability remain available.
- Service Worker/MDM/push/API can retain/distribute recovery material but cannot elect custodians or establish organizational authority.
