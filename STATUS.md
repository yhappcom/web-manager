## 115 material findings
- a suspect offline PWA is a state machine whose observation can mutate evidence: launch, reconnect, navigation, worker inspection/update, export and debugging can change worker/cache/storage/outbox/session/remote state;
- NIST acquisition principles remain useful: identify sources, plan acquisition, acquire, verify integrity, and prioritize by value/volatility/effort. Classic OS ordering must not be copied mechanically into browser/PWA state;
- preserve already-independent remote evidence before reconnecting the suspect client where practical; reconnect is an active state transition, not neutral observation;
- Safari Web Inspector and Chromium DevTools provide useful visibility but are not write-blocked forensic acquisition environments. Exact side effects require engine/version/device validation;
- acquisition and remediation are separate gates. For irreplaceable offline-authoritative data, preserve/classify before clearing storage, unregistering workers or replaying outbox state unless active containment requires an explicit exception;
- hash/digest evidence detects later byte change but does not prove source authenticity, acquisition completeness or semantic integrity;
- operational incident acquisition records/preservation logs must not be mislabeled as formal legal chain of custody. Formal forensic/legal requirements require qualified process and jurisdiction-specific review;
- preserve originals and analyze copies; document every state-changing action and known acquisition-induced mutation;
- user data preservation and replay authorization remain separate facts.
