## 137 checkpoint
- Crypto agility is an operational lifecycle capability, not merely an algorithm/version field. Inventory, deployed verifier capability, transition evidence, retirement policy and validation are required.
- Key rotation, algorithm migration, canonicalization/serialization migration, verifier implementation migration, trust-root rotation and application/Service-Worker generation are separate dimensions.
- Preserve historical verification where justified without preserving obsolete signing/current-write authority. A retired epoch may be verify-only or isolated historical evidence.
- Bind old lineage state to successor verifier/key/algorithm context through explicit authenticated transition evidence; a valid new chain with the same subject is not automatically a continuation.
- Protect current acceptance from downgrade. Client/imported legacy preference cannot reactivate a retired epoch for new writes.
- Planned rotation and compromise recovery are different. Mathematical validity under an old key does not prove an artifact predates key compromise.
- Canonicalization changes can break commitments without changing business semantics; reserialization/re-hashing is a new transformation, not original-signature preservation.
- Legacy verifier retention creates attack surface. If required for historical evidence, scope/isolate it and keep its output explicitly historical rather than current-policy authority.
