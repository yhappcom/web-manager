## 142 checkpoint
- Recovery security and recovery survivability are separate properties. A design can resist abuse yet fail when one owner, approver, credential or provider disappears.
- Study 142 consumes rather than duplicates 126 organizational-loss foundations and applies them to the trust-floor, anti-rollback, compromise-recovery and emergency-reset model established in 137–141.
- No consequence-bearing recovery path should depend irreducibly on one specific human, but continuity must not be achieved by creating a permanently active universal master credential.
- Planned succession is an explicit security transaction: nominate/verify successor, bind bounded capability, activate, prove usability, revoke predecessor, preserve historical verification context, record provenance and test post-transition recovery.
- Credential unavailability and suspected compromise are distinct. Recovering the same possibly compromised credential may restore availability without restoring security.
- Current signing/recovery authority can rotate while historical public verification/provenance material remains available; historical verification continuity does not require obsolete private signing authority.
- Quorum availability depends on failure-domain independence, not headcount alone. Shared IdP/email/password-manager/device/cloud/root/physical custody can correlate nominally separate approvers.
- Emergency material ages: existence, readability, expiry, verifier/tool availability, custody/contact currency, scope and rehearsal status require lifecycle evidence.
- Long-offline PWA/EFB clients do not grandfather old organizational authority. Preserve local irreplaceable data while blocking remote mutation until current authority/trust policy converges.
