## 111 material findings
- recovery is an explicit authority transition, not a weaker ordinary login. Recovery eligibility, emergency activation, domain/provider ownership recovery, trust/key rotation, data reconstruction and destructive retirement should not be collapsed into one standing super-admin path;
- authority continuity is a graph. Registrar recovery through email on the lost domain, cloud recovery through the failed IdP, or trust-root replacement authorized only by a compromised root are circular dependencies;
- domain/DNS/TLS, organizational identity, release signing, application trust policy, sessions/tokens and offline-device authority are separate trust surfaces. Restoring one does not automatically restore the others;
- NIST account-recovery and emergency-account controls support explicit recovery state, notification/audit, least privilege and expiry. Exact MintTap quorum/factors/timing remain product decisions;
- RFC 5011 supplies a useful trust-continuity principle: in-band authenticated rollover works while an accepted root remains trustworthy; total root compromise requires independently governed out-of-band rebootstrap;
- emergency rebootstrap must preserve anti-rollback. Historically authentic revoked keys/credentials/policies are not current authority merely because an old backup or escrow contains them;
- recovery should be staged: declare/freeze/preserve evidence → establish claimant and organizational authority → regain narrow control → establish new authority generation → contain old authority → restore domain/deployment → reconcile stale clients → expire emergency privilege;
- installed PWA state is not repaired merely by restoring the origin. Long-offline clients need generation classification, trust revalidation, safe migration/update, local-data preservation and reconciliation before replay;
- incident closure includes removal/reduction of emergency privilege and review of sessions, tokens, recovery contacts, registrar/DNS/cloud/build/signing privileges and authority graph.

`possession of one recovery factor ≠ full organizational authority proven`.  
`break-glass exists ≠ standing super-admin justified`.  
`documented recovery path ≠ non-circular recovery path`.  
`different factor ≠ independent factor if both recover through the same compromised channel`.  
`DNS restored ≠ registrar ownership restored`.  
`domain possession ≠ release-signing authority`.  
`old root can sign new root ≠ safe recovery if old root is compromised`.  
`automated rollover works normally ≠ total-root-compromise recovery solved`.  
`historically authentic ≠ currently authorized`.  
`emergency reset ≠ anti-rollback reset`.  
`human identity proven ≠ current organizational role proven`.  
`organizational role proven ≠ unrestricted technical authority`.  
`control regained ≠ compromise eradicated`.  
`origin recovered ≠ installed PWA recovered`.  
`TLS valid again ≠ cached worker/data trustworthy`.  
`network reachable ≠ stale client authorized to replay`.  
`incident closed ≠ emergency privilege automatically disappeared`.
