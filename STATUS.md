## 171–199 integrated checkpoint
- Authority, evidence, observation and convergence remain separate layers. Offline-first utility preserves unique local data without making stale authority current.
- Continuity generation issuance, admission, distribution and recovery effectiveness are distinct; conflicting valid-looking heads are incident evidence, not majority elections.
- Continuity-content lineage, admission-policy lineage and admission-key/role lineage remain distinct. Compromised admission material cannot solely bless its successor; unresolved historical intervals remain UNKNOWN through explicit reconstitution.
- Currentness floors survive ordinary control-plane loss in an independent-enough failure domain but remain rollback evidence rather than unilateral authority.
- Reconstitution/break-glass authority is exceptional, incident-bound, scoped and extinguishable; recovery completion requires negative authorization evidence across applicable API/session/region/PITR paths.
- Recovery authorization evidence, emergency identity and drill evidence are distinct assurance objects. Emergency-identity independence is failure-domain-specific and drill assurance is layered rather than a successful-login boolean.
- Evidence authenticity, ceremony purpose, sequence completeness, currentness and actor authorization are separate. Signed drill/recovery evidence can still be replayed, selectively omitted or spliced unless the ceremony model binds purpose, lineage, scope and expected closure.
- Emergency-custody succession is an authorization transition, not additive roster maintenance. Strong handoff requires both successor readiness and former-custodian extinction across relevant account/authenticator/session/provider-recovery paths.
- Material rotation, authenticator invalidation, session invalidation and effective authority extinction are distinct. IdP-session termination does not imply RP-session termination.
- Authority retirement is modeled as an architecture-specific derivation graph across account, authenticator, session/token, shared material, provider recovery, device, regional cache, automation and PITR paths—not as a credential checklist.
- Orphan discovery is asymmetric evidence: finding an orphan proves a defect; finding none proves only exercised discovery coverage. Unresolved paths remain explicitly UNKNOWN or bounded rather than silently becoming INVALIDATED.
- **Revocation requested, accepted, observed and enforced are distinct. Distributed authority retirement is a convergence claim over explicit consequence-bearing enforcement surfaces, not a boolean inferred from one revocation/logout API success.**
- **Residual authority is classified as actively revocable, verified bounded-expiry, or UNKNOWN. `short-lived` is not equivalent to `revoked`, and bounded authority requires a post-expiry negative oracle before scoped convergence is claimed.**
- **Scoped convergence requires the retirement generation/floor, enumerated enforcement domains, negative retired-authority oracles, positive current-authority oracles, residual bounds and unresolved UNKNOWNs. It never proves that no hidden authority copy exists.**
- Long-offline PWA clients preserve local records, obtain current authenticated successor policy/custody/recovery/retirement state, rebootstrap forward and re-admit consequence-bearing work. Cached personnel/session state never becomes current authority.

## Freshness / current external precedent
- NIST SP 800-53 Rev.5 remains current; Release 5.2.0 was issued 2025-08-27. AC/account, AU/audit and PS/personnel lifecycle controls remain useful generic precedent; they do not define MintTap recovery topology or provider-specific invalidation.
- NIST SP 800-53A Rev.5 remains the current assessment methodology; Release 5.2.0 was issued 2025-08-27.
- NIST SP 800-63B-4 is the current final authentication/authenticator/session baseline (2025) and supersedes SP 800-63B. It distinguishes authenticator lifecycle from sessions and states that IdP and RP sessions are independently managed in federation.
- **RFC 9700 / BCP 240 (January 2025) is the current OAuth 2.0 Security Best Current Practice used here for refresh-token replay/rotation, sender constraint and least-privilege token guidance.**
- **RFC 7009 establishes token revocation but permits deployment-specific cascading behavior; RFC 7662 explicitly exposes the authorization-freshness risk of cached introspection responses. These are protocol precedents, not evidence that MintTap uses OAuth.**
- **OpenID Connect RP-Initiated Logout, Session Management, Front-Channel Logout and Back-Channel Logout are Final Specifications; Back-Channel Logout has approved errata. Protocol availability does not establish provider/RP implementation or propagation success.**
- NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-05 remains useful current precedent for policy-governed access review and prompt rescission after role/employment changes.
- NIST SP 800-61 Rev.3 was finalized 2025-04-03 and supersedes Rev.2; use Rev.3 as the current incident-response baseline.
- Current Microsoft Entra emergency-access guidance remains a vendor-specific precedent for separate emergency access/custody, monitoring, validation and rechecking/changing custody after staff departure or role change. Do not generalize its account count, cadence or credential implementation into MintTap requirements without product/provider evidence.
- NIST SP 800-209 Rev.1 remains an Initial Public Draft dated 2026-07-22; treat as CHANGE WATCH, not final baseline.
- NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev.5 remains the final general key-management baseline. Rev.6 is an Initial Public Draft (2025-12-05) and CHANGE WATCH only.
