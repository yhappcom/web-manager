## 138 checkpoint
- Compromise is not ordinary rotation/expiry. Incident scoping, containment/revocation, historical-evidence classification, successor bootstrap, fleet convergence and normalization are distinct phases.
- A mathematically valid old-key signature does not establish that the artifact existed before compromise. Do not infer signing time from an untrusted timestamp field.
- Track last independently trusted evidence, earliest plausible compromise, detection, containment/revocation and successor-trust activation separately. Do not invent an exact compromise instant.
- Revocation limits future/current reliance but does not reconstruct which historical old-key artifacts were genuine.
- A compromised trust authority must not be the sole authority for its own successor. Successor trust requires a sufficiently independent recovery authority/failure domain for the declared threat model.
- Preserve incident evidence without preserving compromised live authority. Historical public verification context and compromised private signing authority have different lifecycle needs.
- Long-offline PWA clients may miss revocation/rebootstrap. Preserve irreplaceable local data while separating local read/export from current remote mutation authority.
- Recovery authority is itself a high-value attack surface; constrain scope, preserve recovery provenance and validate rollback/replay resistance.
