## 086 material findings
- WebSocket is a browser↔server bidirectional transport; it does not supply nearby-device discovery or unattended background execution.
- WebTransport is now a current HTTP/3 client↔server transport with reliable streams/unreliable datagrams; it is not arbitrary direct peer discovery. Current cross-browser behavior remains CHANGE WATCH on exact target versions.
- RTCDataChannel supplies encrypted peer-to-peer arbitrary-data transport and is broadly available, but WebRTC support does not supply a universal application signaling/discovery mechanism, trusted pairing or background persistence.
- `transport supported ≠ peer discovered ≠ endpoint reachable ≠ intended peer authenticated ≠ background execution ≠ synchronization correct`.
- TLS/DTLS protects an established channel but does not prove the remote endpoint is the user's intended paired phone; pairing/revocation/replacement require an application trust contract.
- Generic Safari/WebKit capability cannot establish managed-EFB feasibility; exact OS/WebKit/MDM/network topology, suspension/reconnect and credential persistence require target-device evidence.
- Direct transport, if validated, is an opportunistic transport inside the durability architecture, not the durability foundation.
- Correctness remains `transactional local save → durable outbox → available transport opportunity → authenticated/idempotent apply → acknowledgement → reconciliation`, with independent backup/restore.
- Candidate architecture classes are server/cloud relay, direct WebRTC after establishment, and user-mediated export/import; they can coexist and must be compared against requirements rather than novelty.
