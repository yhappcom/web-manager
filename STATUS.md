## 113 material findings
- restoring domain/origin/deployment authority does not establish installed-client cleanliness because Service Worker registrations persist and controlled clients can remain tied to an active worker generation;
- remediation is an observed client transition, not a server-side publication event: clean worker published ≠ fetched ≠ activated ≠ client revalidated;
- compromise scope is multi-plane: origin exposure, worker/resource generation, session/credential authority, local-data mutation, queued operations, remote side effects and post-remediation trust must be classified separately;
- non-exposure proof and remediation proof are distinct. A remediated client need not be represented as never exposed, and lack of non-exposure evidence does not justify destroying local records;
- Clear-Site-Data can clear IndexedDB, Service Worker registrations and other origin storage, so a broad kill switch can conflict directly with an offline-first EFB whose unique authoritative records live locally;
- incident response should separate executable/resource, authority, domain-data and operation planes: freeze/revoke uncertain authority, preserve data, replace executable state, rebootstrap trust, reconcile operations, then resume replay;
- arbitrary hostile same-origin execution means parseable local bytes are not retrospective integrity proof. Independent record/history/acknowledgement evidence is product-specific and remains OPEN;
- trust re-entry should move monotonically to a new authorized generation rather than reviving a historically valid but potentially compromised generation;
- Web standard/Chromium/Safari/WebKit/managed-iPad behavior must remain separated. Generic Service Worker or Clear-Site-Data support is not target-EFB validation.
