## Current integrated checkpoint — through 280
- Composite `NORMAL` remains derived/revocable; historical/cached decisions do not become independent trust roots.
- Migration completion is consequence closure, not fleet simultaneity; offline/unknown clients may remain if stale authority cannot create material effects without current re-admission.
- Credential inventory is a lineage-and-acceptance graph; acceptance-boundary completeness is a maintained/versioned assurance claim.
- Topology evidence separates **authenticity, accuracy, authority and completeness**. A valid signature does not prove the statement accurate, authorized to normalize a path, or complete.
- Material change lineage should connect authorized intent, source/config revision, produced/deployed artifact, target/generation, observed effect, consequence classification and verifier/policy generation where applicable.
- Discovery sources are evidence channels, not authority-election channels. Poisoned discovery includes omission, false addition and materiality misclassification.
- Contradictory evidence remains first-class state; last-write-wins, newest-source-wins and majority-of-correlated-feeds are not generic adjudication rules.
- Attestation/verifier policy is consequence-bearing authority. A compromised attester/control plane cannot self-clear by issuing a fresh signed `NORMAL` statement.
- **Assurance independence is now modeled as a threat/consequence-specific vector, not a boolean or source count.** Different APIs, keys or vendors can remain correlated through IdP, provider administration, CI/CD, personnel, policy, storage or recovery domains.
- An **independence budget** is a reviewable accounting of which failure-domain separations a consequence class requires; it is not a universal numeric score. Budget assumptions must be re-evaluated after provider/IdP/personnel/recovery changes.
- Correlated agreement is not corroboration. If all material evidence collapses under the active compromise hypothesis, the relevant assurance state is UNKNOWN even when every feed agrees.
- Assurance-plane compromise and protected-system compromise are distinct scopes. Preserve suspect evidence and unique user data while removing current high-consequence authority where necessary.
- The compromised assurance plane cannot certify its own successor. Successor verifier/attester admission requires a trust basis outside the compromised assurance closure or a governed rebootstrap that survives that hypothesis.
- Recovery closure requires successor positive proof **and** predecessor/suspect-authority negative proof; a working V2 alone does not retire V1.
- Restore/PITR is an assurance-plane mutation event: old verifier policy, signer mappings, independence maps and cached `NORMAL` decisions do not regain current authority merely because restored bytes verify.
- Service Worker/client-runtime evidence can challenge server claims but is not an organizational recovery root. Long-offline PWA rejoin preserves unique local records/provenance while seeking current admission and operation-level reconciliation.
